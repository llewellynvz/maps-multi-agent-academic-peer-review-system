A Randomised Trial of a Gratitude Intervention on Subjective Wellbeing

Abstract
This trial evaluates whether a two-week gratitude journalling task raises subjective wellbeing relative to an active control.

Introduction
Gratitude interventions are among the most studied positive psychology exercises, yet replication evidence remains mixed.

Methods
Participants were randomised to the gratitude task or a daily-activities control and completed wellbeing measures before and after the intervention.

Results
The gratitude group showed a small but statistically significant increase in subjective wellbeing.

Discussion
The effect is consistent with prior work, though the short follow-up window limits claims about durability.
